Climograph Generator

This entry describes my journey to produce a working climograph generator for anywhere in the world.

May 10, 2025

I was first introduced to the climograph during my freshman year physical geography class as a way to illustrate the major variables differentiating climates. I was fascinated by it and its ability to visually explain what a location's concomitant Köppen climate type really meant.

This interest led me to create my first in a series of climograph generators using NOAA's Global Historical Climatology Network (GHCN). Working with this source of data proved to be rather difficult, however, stemming from the extremely diverse array of station time series coverages. As such, creating a global set of climate data from this source would result in graphs being incomporable due to being averaged out from different date ranges as well as having climographs generated from the nearest station. In areas with high coverage this latter stipulation would likely be insignificant, but in remote regions it could very well be over a hundred miles to the nearest station with NOAA records.

I pivoted towards using a raster source from WorldClim with a resolution of 10 minutes, providing a compromise between over- and under-represented areas arising from my first approach. The raster solution proved to be more straightforward, although I quickly came to find issues in the support of the fundamental climograph structure in ggplot2. The design philosophy expounded by the developers of ggplot2 is one that rejects secondary axes due to them generally being a poor statistical practice. With this I must agree, but it must also be noted that the secondary axis is vital to a climograph as the relationship between temperature and precipitation with regard to yearly variation is the most important component of climate. I found a work-around, however, by rescaling the actual values of temperature. This solution works well for most places, but appears to exaggerate the difference in yearly temperature for equatorial regions with consistent temperatures due to how I compute the range.
